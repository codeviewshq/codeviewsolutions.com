## Design Specificity Verdict - strongly authored, wrong register

Not category-interchangeable. Zone rail, sheet-edge registration marks, clause rows with a
parameter third column, a rate schedule that refuses to be pricing cards, a terminal issue block.
The default gradient-hero-over-three-cards is genuinely absent. Only interchangeable element found
site-wide: the four-cell competency grid on About.

The problem is register, not sameness. PRODUCT.md is explicit that jargon builds unease with this
buyer. The site swapped software jargon for drafting-office jargon and called it plain language.
The prose respects the reader; the furniture does not.

## Overall impression

Genuinely authored design losing the sale in the last ten feet. It answers "are these people any
good" magnificently and answers "can I afford this" on the fifth screen, when PRODUCT.md says the
money question arrives first. The journey ends in a valley: the visitor arrives at contact already
convinced and is told twice, in red, to go somewhere that does not exist.

Biggest opportunity: invert the order of reassurance. "$10 a month, after a free design" in the
first viewport would do more work than anything else on the site.

## What's Working

1. The rate schedule (sheet 05) works because it refused to be pricing cards. Three columns share
one navy ground separated by gap-as-rule; no tier elevated, no "most popular" badge, no scaled
middle column. For a buyer whose first unspoken question is "can I afford this," the absence of
upsell choreography IS the persuasion. Prices in the dimension ramp with tabular-nums, fenced by
keylines, so $10 reads as a measured fact. Both cycles in the DOM; correct with JS off.

2. The clause list beats the card grid on substance. Each row carries a mono figure, name, summary,
AND a three-row parameter table. "Good fit for: businesses without web staff" lets a visitor
self-qualify in one glance. A card grid has nowhere to put that. The conceit produced better IA.

3. The closing issue block earns being the only dark field. Five sheets refusing fills for hierarchy
make the reversal read as terminal. The four-cell sign-off restates the four commitments a nervous
buyer needs at the moment of decision, as a contract stamp rather than a bullet list.
